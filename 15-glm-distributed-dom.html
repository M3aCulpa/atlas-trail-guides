Willow Creek Trail Guide

Maintained by the Willow Creek Conservancy. Last updated October 2024.

Trail Overview

The Willow Creek Loop is a moderate 6.4-mile trail that winds through old-growth forest along Willow Creek before climbing to a ridge with panoramic views of the valley. It is suitable for most hikers and dogs on leash.

What to Bring

  • Sturdy hiking boots (the creek crossing can be slippery)
  • 1.5 liters of water per person minimum
  • Trail snacks: nuts, dried fruit, energy bars
  • Rain jacket (weather changes quickly in the valley)
  • Sun protection: hat, sunscreen, sunglasses
  • Map or GPS (cell service is spotty past mile 2)

Trail Segments

Segment 1: Trailhead to Creek Crossing (1.2 mi)

An easy walk through meadow and into the forest. The path is wide and well-maintained. You will cross two small footbridges before reaching the main creek crossing at mile 1.2.

Segment 2: Creek Crossing to Ridge (2.8 mi)

The steepest section. You gain 900 feet over switchbacks through dense forest. Look for the giant Douglas fir at mile 2.5, estimated at over 400 years old.

Segment 3: Ridge to Trailhead (2.4 mi)

The reward: a mile along the ridge with open views before a gentle descent back to the parking area. Best light for photography is in the late afternoon.

WCC maintains this trail with volunteer crews every spring and fall. Trail conditions are updated weekly during peak season.

Safety Notes

Bears are occasionally seen on the trail. Make noise on blind corners and carry bear spray from June through October. The creek crossing can be dangerous during spring snowmelt; check water levels before heading out.

In winter, the ridge section is exposed and can be icy. Microspikes are recommended from December through March.

Getting There

From downtown, take Highway 20 west for 12 miles. Turn right on Forest Road 4200 and continue 3 miles to the trailhead parking area. The last mile is gravel but passable for all vehicles.
