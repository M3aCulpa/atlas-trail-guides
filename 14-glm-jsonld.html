Heirloom Apple Pie

By Margaret Hale. Updated October 2024

Five Generations of Friday Dessert

This pie first appeared in our family in 1911, when great-great-grandmother Ada Hale adapted her mother's dried-apple hand pie into a full-sized beauty for the county fair. She won a blue ribbon that year and every year after until she stopped entering in 1938. The recipe passed to her daughter Lillian, then to my grandmother Ruth, my mother Jean, and finally to me.

The secret is restraint. Just enough sugar to balance the Granny Smiths. Just enough spice to warm the filling without masking the fruit. And absolutely no cornstarch; flour thickens more gently and lets the apple flavor come through clean.

Ingredients

For the Filling

  • 6 cups Granny Smith apples (about 6 medium), peeled, cored, sliced 1/4-inch thick
  • 2 tablespoons fresh lemon juice
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon freshly grated nutmeg
  • 1 tablespoon cold unsalted butter, diced small

For the Crust

  • 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup (2 sticks) cold unsalted butter, cut into 1/2-inch cubes
  • 6 to 8 tablespoons ice water

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 425F with a rack on the lowest position.
  2. Toss apple slices with lemon juice. Add sugar, flour, cinnamon, and nutmeg; toss until evenly coated. Let sit 15 minutes while you roll the crust.
  3. Roll the bottom crust to a 12-inch round. Fit into a 9-inch pie plate, leaving a 1-inch overhang.
  4. Pour the filling into the crust. Dot with the diced butter.
  5. Roll the top crust to an 11-inch round. Lay over the filling. Trim to a 3/4-inch overhang, fold the edges under, and crimp. Cut four 2-inch slits in the top for steam.
  6. Bake 20 minutes at 425F. Reduce heat to 375F and bake 30 to 35 minutes more, until the crust is deep golden and juices bubble through the vents.
  7. Cool on a wire rack at least 3 hours before slicing. The filling needs this time to set; cutting earlier gives a runny interior.

Tips

Use a mix of tart and sweet apples for more complexity. Granny Smith is classic, but adding two Honeycrisp gives a rounder sweetness. Keep the butter ice cold; the flaky layers depend on pockets of cold butter melting in the oven.
